# Manuscript Engineering M001 — Gold Standard

Evaluator-only. Do not send this file to the model under test.

## Overall reconstruction

*The Bell at Low Tide* is a five-chapter present-day investigation built around Mara Vale's return to Bellwether after her mother's death and the unresolved disappearance of her younger brother Jonah twelve years earlier.

Mara initially wants only to clear and sell the signal house. A sequence of radio transmissions, archival records, objects and confessions reopens the disappearance. The investigation first appears to centre on Gate Four and a storm-era municipal report, then broadens into evidence that Jonah had been investigating night dumping near the culvert and that the official account of his route was false. The manuscript ends with stronger evidence about Jonah's location and what he was investigating, but **does not resolve Jonah's fate, the identity of the radio voice, the identity of `D`, or the full extent of Elise/Devlin/Peter Rusk's knowledge and responsibility**.

Mara withdraws the house from sale, indicating a changed relationship to Bellwether/the investigation, but the ending remains open.

## Chapter map

### Chapter 1

Explicit events/reveals:
- Mara returns after Elise's death to empty and sell the signal house.
- Jonah disappeared twelve years earlier during the September storm.
- Devlin/council has interest in land behind the property for seawall access.
- Mara finds an old radio and a maintenance log with a page torn out.
- Last pre-gap log entry: `21:46 — storm warning upgraded; Gate Four remote sensor intermittent.`
- Radio battery area feels warm despite closed curtains; Mara offers an unverified sun explanation.
- Radio emits three short bursts and one long burst.
- Childhood Jonah used three knocks on Mara's wall; this is suggestive but **does not identify the radio signal as Jonah**.

State change: Mara is emotionally pulled toward the old disappearance despite insisting she came only to sell.

### Chapter 2

Explicit events/reveals:
- Theo Rusk helps repair radio; his father Peter was chief municipal engineer during the old storm.
- Elise had been in hospital for nine days, complicating Theo's possible recent-charging explanation; cause of battery warmth remains Unknown.
- Distorted voice says `Mara, don't let them open Gate Four.`
- Mara says it sounded like Jonah but explicitly says she does not know the speaker.
- Elise's map note reads `J knew. D lied.` Identity/referent of J/D is not established by the note alone.
- Public storm report says Gate Four manually opened at 22:17 after remote sensor failure; operator initials `E.V.`
- Mara interprets `E.V.` as Elise Vale; Theo accurately qualifies this as what the report says.
- Later summary says Jonah was seventeen and police believed he rode north toward ridge road.
- Technical appendix has different typeface; this is observable, but reason/significance is not yet established.

### Chapter 3

Explicit events/reveals:
- Mara/Theo enter abandoned pumphouse using Theo's old service key.
- Mara finds Jonah's red scarf in an unlabelled evidence bag; she recognises it from family history.
- Tape contains Elise saying `You said the road was clear`, unnamed man answering `It was supposed to be`, then Elise saying `Jonah was out there.`
- The man's identity remains Unknown.
- Theo confesses he lied to police: he saw Jonah cycling south toward pumphouse, not north toward ridge.
- Theo says Peter Rusk ordered him to keep quiet; Peter's reason is not established.
- Theo says both he and Jonah were sixteen; report says Jonah was seventeen. This is an explicit age discrepancy that the text itself leaves unresolved.

State change: official route/account is materially undermined; Mara now suspects multiple lies.

### Chapter 4

Explicit events/reveals:
- Devlin acknowledges technical appendix was amended.
- Devlin says Peter Rusk rewrote it after insurance review; Devlin signed the summary.
- Devlin's stated rationale is keeping the town functioning amid lawsuits; this is Devlin's account, not independent proof of motive.
- Devlin denies having seen Jonah that night but does not directly answer the `D lied` accusation.
- Devlin says Elise opened Gate Four after sensor failure.
- Devlin remembers opening time about 22:14, while archive says 22:17; he tells Mara to trust archive over memory. This is a timing discrepancy, not proof of deception.
- Devlin says a road-closure message was never relayed but says he does not know why.
- Present-day Gate Four malfunctions during new storm; Mara shifts to evacuation/help.
- Later radio message says `Not the gate. The culvert.` The voice sounds same or similar through static, but identity remains Unknown.
- Old plan reveals disused culvert under Drowned Road toward quarry boundary.

### Chapter 5

Explicit events/reveals:
- Mara, Theo and rescue workers enter culvert.
- They find Jonah's bicycle; Mara identifies the bell.
- No human remains are found.
- Waterproof notebook bearing Jonah's name contains entries about late-night trucks dumping sealed drums near culvert.
- Jonah copied three registration numbers and wrote `RUSK CIVIL` beside two.
- Jonah planned to photograph trucks.
- Last entry: `Mum found me. She says we have to go before D sees.`
- Text explicitly refuses to equate D with Devlin or anyone else.
- Notebook establishes Jonah was at culvert and Elise found him there; does not establish what happened next.
- Mara withdraws signal house from sale.
- Radio remains silent in final scene.

## Character / relationship ground truth

### Mara Vale
Explicit:
- Jonah's older sister; Elise's daughter.
- Left Bellwether after Jonah disappeared; returns after Elise dies.
- Initial stated goal: clear/sell house within a week.
- Later acts to investigate Jonah's disappearance and ultimately withdraws property from sale.

Do not invent:
- exact reason she left beyond what text states;
- diagnosis/trauma labels;
- certainty that she now plans to remain permanently.

### Jonah Vale
Explicit:
- Mara's younger brother; disappeared twelve years earlier.
- Was near culvert on disappearance night; notebook indicates he was investigating dumping and Elise found him.
- Bicycle and scarf are found; no body/remains found.

Unknown:
- whether alive/dead;
- what happened after Elise found him;
- whether he sent any present-day radio transmission.

### Elise Vale
Explicit:
- Mara/Jonah's mother; deceased in present.
- `E.V.` appears as Gate Four operator initials in report and Mara interprets this as her.
- Her voice is on tape.
- Jonah's notebook says `Mum found me`.
- Wrote `J knew. D lied.`

Unknown:
- exact knowledge of dumping before finding Jonah;
- why Gate Four was opened beyond accounts in report/Devlin's statement;
- what she did with Jonah after finding him.

### Theo Rusk
Explicit:
- Mara's childhood friend; harbour mechanic; Peter's son.
- Helps Mara investigate.
- Lied to police as a teenager about Jonah's direction because his father ordered silence.

Unknown/interpretive:
- guilt/atonement are plausible interpretations but not explicit stated motives.

### Peter Rusk
Explicit:
- Former chief municipal engineer; Theo's father.
- Ordered Theo to keep quiet according to Theo.
- Rewrote technical appendix according to Devlin.
- `RUSK CIVIL` appears beside two truck plate numbers in Jonah's notebook.

Unknown:
- ownership/relationship of Peter to RUSK CIVIL is not explicitly stated.
- whether Peter participated in dumping.
- why he ordered Theo quiet.

### Daniel Devlin
Explicit:
- Mayor in present.
- Says Peter rewrote appendix; Devlin signed summary.
- Does not directly answer whether `D` refers to him or whether he lied.
- Says he never saw Jonah that night.

Unknown:
- whether `D` is Devlin.
- whether he knew about dumping.
- degree of culpability beyond his own acknowledged signing of amended summary.

## Chronology / causal ground truth

Present action spans roughly four days:
- Day 1: Mara returns; finds log/radio; signal bursts.
- Day 2: Theo repairs radio; voice message; archive research.
- Day 3: rain/storm; pumphouse discovery/confession; Devlin meeting; evacuation; second voice message.
- Day 4 after storm: culvert search; notebook/bicycle found; Mara withdraws sale.

Twelve years earlier:
- Storm warning upgraded at 21:46; Gate Four sensor intermittent.
- Gate Four reported opened at 22:17; Devlin remembers about 22:14.
- Theo saw Jonah heading south toward pumphouse and later lied to police under Peter's instruction.
- Jonah had been investigating dumping near culvert.
- Jonah's last note says Elise found him and wanted to leave before `D` saw.
- Elise tape places Jonah `out there` in context of a road-clearance dispute, but exact timestamp of tape is not supplied.
- What happened to Jonah after Elise found him remains unresolved.

Causality that **is not established**:
- Gate Four opening caused Jonah's disappearance.
- Peter Rusk/RUSK CIVIL caused Jonah's disappearance.
- Devlin caused Jonah's disappearance.
- dumping caused Jonah's disappearance.
- radio transmissions are supernatural or sent by Jonah.

## Key uncertainty register

Must remain unresolved:
1. identity/source of radio transmissions;
2. meaning/identity of `J` and especially `D` in Elise's note;
3. identity of male voice on cassette;
4. reason for warm battery area;
5. exact reason Peter ordered Theo to lie;
6. who owned/operated `RUSK CIVIL` and what its involvement was;
7. Devlin's knowledge of dumping and whether `D` means Devlin;
8. what happened after Elise found Jonah;
9. Jonah's fate;
10. whether 22:14 vs 22:17 is merely memory variance or substantively important;
11. whether Jonah was sixteen or seventeen at disappearance.

## Motifs / possible themes

High-confidence motifs because repeated text supports them:
- radio/static/signals;
- water/storm/tide/drainage;
- missing/altered records (torn log page, amended report, blank notebook pages);
- gates/roads/culverts and blocked or redirected passage;
- sound/signalling (radio bursts, harbour bell, old knock pattern).

Reasonable themes but must be labelled interpretation, not authorial fact:
- memory versus official record;
- personal guilt and inherited silence;
- institutional concealment / compromised public truth;
- returning home to confront unresolved history;
- uncertainty and the limits of evidence.

## Continuity / discrepancy ground truth

Expected flags:
- Jonah age: report says seventeen; Theo remembers Jonah as sixteen. Text acknowledges discrepancy and does not resolve it.
- Gate opening time: archive says 22:17; Devlin remembers about 22:14 and defers to archive. Preserve as conflicting record/memory.
- radio voice: first message and second may be same or similar; text deliberately does not guarantee identity.

Do **not** falsely flag as contradiction:
- Elise being in hospital nine days does not prove she could not have charged radio earlier; it only weakens a recent-charge explanation.
- different report typeface is not itself proof of fraudulent alteration, though later Devlin confirms amendment.
- no remains alongside bicycle does not prove Jonah survived.

## Living editorial-brief seed

A correct seed should neutrally capture:
- mystery/investigation premise;
- Mara's movement from liquidation/detachment toward renewed investigation and withholding sale;
- Gate Four as an apparent centre of the mystery that later redirects toward culvert/dumping;
- official-report reliability concerns and Theo's false police account;
- confirmed Jonah notebook evidence;
- unresolved radio source, `D`, culpability, and Jonah fate;
- the open ending.

It should list author-confirmation items rather than deciding them. Appropriate confirmation questions include whether the radio source, `D`, Jonah's fate, age discrepancy and timing discrepancy are intentionally unresolved/ambiguous and whether any should become fixed canon before chapter editing.

## Forbidden reconstruction errors

- identifying the radio voice as Jonah as fact;
- identifying `D` as Devlin as fact;
- identifying cassette male voice as Devlin or Peter as fact;
- asserting Jonah is dead/alive;
- asserting Peter owns RUSK CIVIL without textual proof;
- asserting Gate Four caused Jonah's disappearance;
- treating plausible guilt/atonement themes as explicit motive;
- silently fixing age/time discrepancies;
- proposing rewrites, chapter moves or editorial fixes in this benchmark.
