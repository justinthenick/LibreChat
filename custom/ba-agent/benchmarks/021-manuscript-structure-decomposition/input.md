# Synthetic manuscript: *The Bell at Low Tide*

This is a deliberately small five-chapter fiction manuscript for structural analysis. Treat the text itself as the only source of truth. Do not browse or add outside facts.

---

## Chapter 1 — The Bell at Low Tide

Mara Vale returned to Bellwether because there was no one else to empty her mother's house.

The signal house stood above the harbour on a slope salted white by wind. Twelve years earlier, Mara's younger brother Jonah had vanished during the September storm. Their mother, Elise, had remained in Bellwether until her death; Mara had not.

Mara told the estate agent she wanted the place cleared and sold within a week. The agent warned her that Mayor Daniel Devlin had already asked whether the council might purchase the strip of land behind the house for the new seawall access road. Mara said she did not care who bought it.

In Elise's study, she found the old harbour radio under a dust sheet. Beside it lay a maintenance log with one page torn out. The last complete entry before the gap was dated the night Jonah disappeared: `21:46 — storm warning upgraded; Gate Four remote sensor intermittent.` The next surviving page began the following morning.

Mara touched the radio casing. It was cold except around the battery compartment, which felt faintly warm. She assumed the afternoon sun had reached it through the window, although the curtains were still closed.

She switched it on. Static filled the room, then three short bursts and one long burst sounded through the speaker.

When they were children, Jonah had knocked three times on Mara's bedroom wall whenever he wanted her to sneak downstairs after lights-out. She stared at the radio until the signal dissolved back into static.

That evening, standing by the harbour bell, Mara told herself she had come to sell a house, not reopen a disappearance.

---

## Chapter 2 — Static

Theo Rusk arrived the next morning with a toolbox and two takeaway coffees. He and Mara had grown up together; now he repaired engines at the harbour yard. His father, Peter Rusk, had been Bellwether's chief municipal engineer during the storm twelve years earlier.

Theo replaced a corroded fuse in the radio power supply. He said the battery warmth was strange but not impossible if Elise had charged the unit shortly before she died. Mara reminded him Elise had been in hospital for nine days. Theo did not answer.

The radio came alive at 10:12.

A distorted voice said, `Mara, don't let them open Gate Four.`

Mara went rigid. Theo asked whether she recognised the speaker.

`I don't know`, she said. Then, after a moment: `It sounded like him.`

The transmission did not repeat.

In Elise's desk drawer, Mara found a folded council map. Gate Four was marked in red where the old drainage system met the harbour wall. In the margin, Elise had written: `J knew. D lied.` No other explanation appeared on the map.

Mara and Theo went to the municipal archive. The public storm report stated that Gate Four had been manually opened at 22:17 on the night Jonah vanished after the remote sensor failed. The operator initials were `E.V.`

`Mum opened it`, Mara said.

Theo replied, `That's what the report says.`

A later summary attached to the file described Jonah as seventeen years old and recorded that police believed he had ridden north toward the ridge road before the worst flooding began.

On the way out, Mara noticed that the technical appendix had a different typeface from the original incident pages. Theo said council reports were often reformatted after review. Mara photographed both sections.

---

## Chapter 3 — The Drowned Road

Rain arrived before dawn on the third day.

Mara and Theo followed the drainage map to the abandoned pumphouse beside Drowned Road. The building had been locked since the council installed the new pumps, but Theo still had an old service key from his apprenticeship.

Inside, Mara found a rusted locker labelled `TOOLS / G4`. A red wool scarf had been sealed inside a clear evidence bag with no police label. Mara recognised it as Jonah's; their mother had knitted two matching scarves the winter before he disappeared.

Theo said he had never seen the bag before.

A cassette recorder sat on a shelf below the locker. The tape inside contained less than a minute of usable sound. Elise's voice said, `You said the road was clear.`

A man's voice answered, `It was supposed to be.`

Elise said, `Jonah was out there.`

The rest was drowned by mechanical noise. Neither speaker named the man.

Outside, Theo stopped Mara before they reached the road.

`I lied to the police`, he said.

He told her that on the night Jonah disappeared he had seen Jonah cycling south toward the pumphouse, not north toward the ridge. Theo said his father ordered him to keep quiet and told him that repeating what he saw would only make things worse.

`Why would your father care where Jonah went?`

`I don't know. I was sixteen. Jonah was sixteen. I did what Dad told me.`

Mara stared at him. `The report says Jonah was seventeen.`

Theo rubbed rain from his face. `Then either I remember his age wrong or the report does.`

For the first time, Mara considered that the official story might have been built from more than one lie.

---

## Chapter 4 — Gate Four

Mayor Devlin agreed to meet Mara in the council emergency room because the new storm warning had closed the public offices.

Mara placed her photographs of the report on the table and asked why the appendix looked newer than the incident pages.

Devlin did not deny that the report had been amended.

`Peter Rusk rewrote the technical appendix after the insurance review`, he said. `I signed the summary. We were trying to keep the town functioning. There were lawsuits everywhere.`

Mara asked whether he had lied about Jonah.

`I never saw Jonah that night.`

`My mother wrote D lied.`

Devlin looked at the map but did not answer the accusation directly.

He said Elise had opened Gate Four after the remote sensor failed. According to his memory, the gate opened at about 22:14. He said a road-closure message that should have gone to the harbour crew was never relayed, but he did not know why.

`The archive says 22:17`, Mara said.

`Then trust the archive over my memory`, Devlin replied.

Before Mara could ask more, an alarm sounded. Gate Four was failing to respond to the current storm controls, and water was rising along the lower harbour.

Mara left the meeting to help Theo and the volunteer crews move residents from two flooded streets. The search for answers stopped for six hours.

At 19:33, back at the signal house, the radio activated by itself.

The same distorted voice—or a voice that sounded similar through the static—said, `Not the gate. The culvert.`

Theo found an older drainage plan. It showed a disused service culvert running beneath Drowned Road from the pumphouse toward the quarry boundary.

---

## Chapter 5 — Under the Road

The storm passed overnight.

The next morning, Mara, Theo and two council rescue workers entered the culvert from the quarry side. Thirty metres in, they found the bent frame of a bicycle wedged behind fallen masonry. Mara identified the faded silver bell on the handlebars as Jonah's.

No human remains were found.

Behind the bicycle was a small metal cashbox wrapped in two plastic bags. Inside was a waterproof notebook with Jonah's name on the first page.

Most entries were ordinary: school complaints, sketches of boats, lists of songs. The final pages changed tone. Jonah wrote that trucks had been entering the quarry service road after midnight and dumping sealed drums near the culvert. He had copied three registration numbers and written `RUSK CIVIL` beside two of them. He planned to photograph the trucks because `nobody listens when you only tell them.`

The last written entry was dated the night he disappeared:

`Mum found me. She says we have to go before D sees.`

The remaining pages were blank.

Theo read the line twice and sat down against the culvert wall.

Mara did not say that D meant Devlin. She did not say it meant anyone else.

The notebook established that Jonah had been at the culvert and that Elise had found him there. It did not explain what happened next.

Back at the signal house, Mara called the estate agent and withdrew the property from sale.

That night she left the harbour radio switched on beside the torn maintenance log. No transmission came.

At low tide, the bell in the harbour moved once in the wind and then was still.
