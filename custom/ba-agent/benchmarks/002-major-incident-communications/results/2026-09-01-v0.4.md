# Benchmark 002 — Skill v0.4 Results (2026-09-01)

## Benchmark

**002 — Major Incident Communications Automation**

Purpose: test whether `analyze-requirements` generalizes beyond Benchmark 001 to a different IT service-management problem with external communications, privacy boundaries, volatile incident data, recipient/channel uncertainty and a different stakeholder conflict.

## Results summary

| Model | Baseline (no skill) | `analyze-requirements` v0.4 | Improvement |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Gemini 3.6 Flash | 57/100 | 95/100 | +38 |
| Gemini 3.5 Flash | 60/100 | 81/100 | +21 |

## Gemini 3.6 Flash findings

### Baseline — 57/100

Strengths:
- Preserved the Service Desk auto-send versus Incident Manager confirmation disagreement.
- Identified material-status-change ambiguity, recipient/channel uncertainty and API/data-quality risks.

Weaknesses:
- Weak item-level traceability.
- Hardened tentative Severity 1 scope and the approximate eight-week target.
- Promoted the Agile Lead delivery suggestion toward first-release scope.
- Invented or strengthened trigger behavior beyond supplied evidence.
- Assigned unsupported governance/decision authority.

### Skill v0.4 — 95/100

Strengths:
- Strong item-level traceability.
- Correctly classified Severity 1 as Candidate, eight weeks and five minutes as Target, future templates/multilingual as Deferred, and internal approval as Disputed.
- Preserved `Decision owner: Unknown` for unresolved business-policy decisions.
- Separated required outcomes from analyst-suggested mechanisms.
- Kept unverified integrations as Candidate and feasibility unknown.

Minor remaining issue:
- One role-based authority inference remained around Product Owner backlog prioritization.

## Gemini 3.5 Flash findings

### Baseline — 60/100

Strengths:
- Preserved the core internal approval conflict.
- Identified material-status-change, cadence, recipient/channel and technical-feasibility gaps.

Weaknesses:
- Weak traceability and no formal evidence/status model.
- Severity 1 and the eight-week target were somewhat hardened.
- External-approval wording was weakened compared with the source.
- Familiar job titles were used to infer responsibilities/authority not explicitly established.

### Skill v0.4 — 81/100

Strengths:
- Correct Evidence class / Requirement status behavior for most items.
- Strong traceability and open questions.
- Preserved the disputed internal approval rule.
- Kept candidate integrations and deferred future features separate from confirmed requirements.

Remaining model-execution weaknesses:
- Inferred authority for Incident Manager, Security/Privacy and Agile Lead beyond what the source established.
- Occasionally converted required outcomes into proposed governance/discovery mechanisms.
- Analyst proposals became too prescriptive in places.

## Conclusion

`analyze-requirements` v0.4 is **validated as a useful reusable capability** across two materially different benchmarks.

The same skill materially improved both Gemini 3.6 Flash and Gemini 3.5 Flash, while the stronger model executed the methodology more consistently. This supports treating **skill quality** and **model capability** as separate variables.

Further micro-optimization of Benchmark 001/002 is lower value than expanding the BA agent with a separately benchmarked downstream capability.
