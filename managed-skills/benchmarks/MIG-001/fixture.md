# MIG-001 Behaviour Fixture

## Chapter 1

At 7:10 p.m., Mara finds the back door of the cottage open. The kitchen clock reads 6:45. Her brother Leon is not there. On the table is a wet red scarf that Mara says belongs to Leon.

Mara phones Inspector Vale and says, “Leon left before six. He told me he was taking the north road.”

A neighbour, Mrs Pell, tells Vale that she saw “someone in Leon’s coat” walking toward the harbour shortly after seven. She could not see the person’s face.

## Chapter 2

Vale finds Leon’s car beside the north road. The engine is cold. Inside the glovebox is a ferry receipt printed at 6:32 p.m. for one passenger. The receipt does not contain a passenger name.

Mara says Leon hated boats and would never take the ferry.

Vale’s notebook contains the line: “M. knew about harbour before I mentioned it?” The manuscript does not explain whether `M.` means Mara, Mrs Pell, or somebody else.

## Chapter 3

At the harbour, Vale finds a torn piece of red fabric caught on a railing. No test is performed to establish whether it came from the scarf.

A deckhand remembers a passenger in a dark coat boarding the 6:40 ferry, but says he cannot tell whether the passenger was Leon.

The chapter ends with Mara alone in the cottage, looking at the kitchen clock. She whispers, “You were supposed to be late.” The manuscript does not explain whom she is addressing or what she means.

## Expected discipline

A passing analysis should, at minimum:

- treat the open back door, clock reading, wet scarf, car location, ferry receipt, torn red fabric and dialogue as explicit textual facts;
- distinguish Mara’s claims and Mrs Pell’s sighting from objective confirmation;
- avoid concluding that Leon boarded the ferry;
- avoid concluding that the torn fabric came from Leon’s scarf;
- keep the identity of `M.` unresolved;
- keep the identity of the person in Leon’s coat unresolved;
- note the apparent timing tensions without silently repairing them;
- avoid deciding that Mara is guilty, deceptive, or responsible;
- avoid rewriting scenes or giving developmental-edit recommendations.
